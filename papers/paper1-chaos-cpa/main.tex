\begin{abstract}
A 2025 chaos-based image cipher (Jain \emph{et al.}, \emph{Optik}; Lorenz/logistic maps with
Fisher--Yates block shuffling, ``LLEO'') is completely broken: from \PH{cam_cpt} chosen plaintexts we
recover a key-free decryptor for any $512{\times}512$ image and decrypt any ciphertext pixel-exactly in
\PH{cam_attack_s}\,s, despite the scheme's near-ideal entropy (\PH{cam_ent_cipher}) and reported
$99.6\%$ NPCR. The cause is structural: LLEO's keystream and both permutations depend only on the secret
key, never on the image, so the whole cipher is a fixed, plaintext-independent map. We reconstruct it from
its description (matching the reported entropy and near-zero pixel correlation), recover the per-position
keystream with an all-ones probe and the permutation with $\lceil\log_{256}n\rceil$ index-digit probes,
and show its headline differential metric is illusory: with no plaintext diffusion a one-pixel change
alters exactly one ciphertext pixel, a \emph{true} NPCR of \PH{cam_npcr1px}\,\% ($100/MN$), not the
reported $99.6\%$ (which is merely the difference between unrelated images, \PH{npcr_unrelated}\,\%).

This break stands on the real construction. We then ask how far it generalizes by distilling it into a
black-box \emph{structural oracle} that, given only chosen-image encryption access, decides whether a
cipher is a fixed plaintext-independent map and recovers an equivalent key, certifying each verdict by
predicting held-out ciphertext (we prove the certificate sound). On a corpus of \PH{oracle_n_schemes}
structural archetypes it breaks all \PH{oracle_n_keyonly_broken} fully key-only constructions in at most
\PH{oracle_max_queries} chosen plaintexts, clears the \PH{oracle_n_bound_resist} that bind the keystream
to the plaintext or a nonce, and degrades gracefully on a sixth, non-invertible key-only variant (the
deliberate boundary); on \PH{pub_n_real} \emph{real} published 2023--2025 schemes
(reconstructed from their equations, and shown to reproduce the originals' reported entropy and NPCR) it
breaks the one key-only design and clears three that seed the keystream from the plaintext. Breaking the
key-only scheme---an XOR keystream under a per-pixel cellular-automata stage---needed a GF(2)-affine
(\emph{bitlinear}) extension of the recovery, sound and leaving every prior verdict unchanged. The oracle
covers affine, monomial, XOR, and bit-level substitution families; a key-only but genuinely nonlinear byte
S-box stays structurally broken yet outside this logarithmic-query regime. We close with the standard
fixes.
\end{abstract}

Chaos-based image ciphers appear in the literature at a high rate, and a recurring lesson from the
cryptanalysis side is that a large fraction of them succumb to the same structural attack. Most follow
a permutation--diffusion template: a confusion stage that rearranges pixels and a diffusion stage that
masks pixel values with a chaotic keystream. When that keystream is a function of the secret key alone
and not of the image, the entire cipher collapses to a \emph{fixed} permutation--substitution map,
and an \emph{equivalent key}---a stand-in that decrypts every ciphertext under the same secret key,
without being the key itself---can be recovered from a handful of chosen plaintexts (input images the
attacker picks and has encrypted)~\cite{li2019attacker,li2018entropy,arroyo2009,li2011optimal}. The defence is equally
well known: the keystream must depend on the plaintext, and the cipher must actually
diffuse~\cite{alvarez2006}.

A second, persistent problem is methodological. Authors validate schemes with statistical scores---%
Shannon entropy (a measure of randomness), adjacent-pixel correlation (how alike neighbouring pixels
are), and the differential metrics NPCR and UACI (how much the output changes when one input pixel
changes)---and treat near-ideal values as evidence of security. None of these scores measures resistance to a
chosen-plaintext attack, and, as we show, all of them can be near-ideal for a cipher that is broken in
seconds.

This paper applies both lessons to a 2025 scheme~\cite{jain2025lleo} (which we abbreviate LLEO) built
from the Lorenz~\cite{lorenz1963} and logistic maps with Fisher--Yates block shuffling
(Fig.~\ref{fig:schema}). Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We identify that LLEO is entirely key-driven and therefore a fixed monomial map over
$\mathbb{Z}_{256}$---each output pixel is one input pixel times a fixed multiplier, with all arithmetic
taken modulo 256 (wrapping around like a clock) (Section~\ref{sec:target}--\ref{sec:crypt}).
\item We give an equivalent-key chosen-plaintext attack that recovers the full map in
$1+\lceil\log_{256} MN\rceil$ chosen plaintexts---\PH{cam_cpt} for a $512\times512$ image---and
decrypts any image pixel-exactly without the key (Section~\ref{sec:crypt}).
\item We reproduce LLEO, confirm it matches the paper's reported statistics, and then show those
statistics coexist with \emph{zero} plaintext diffusion: the true one-pixel NPCR is
\PH{cam_npcr1px}\,\%, not 99.6\,\% (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item Most importantly, we distil the attack into a reusable, black-box \emph{structural audit} that
decides plaintext-independence for any cipher, with a held-out certificate we prove sound
(Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}). It recovers an equivalent key from every key-only archetype and
clears those that bind the keystream to the image or a nonce; on \PH{pub_n_real} \emph{real} published
2023--2025 schemes it breaks the one key-only design and clears three plaintext-bound ones
(Section~\ref{sec:oracle}, Table~\ref{tab:published}). Breaking a real bit-level cipher required---and
motivated---a GF(2)-affine extension of the recovery that widens its reach while leaving every prior
verdict intact.
\end{itemize}
Fig.~\ref{fig:break} previews the outcome: four standard images, their LLEO ciphertexts, and our
key-free recoveries, each bit-identical to the original and obtained in \PH{cam_cpt} chosen plaintexts.

\section{Related work}
The equivalent-key methodology for permutation--diffusion ciphers is well established. Arroyo
\emph{et al.}~\cite{arroyo2009} broke a ``total shuffling'' scheme by recovering its key-only
keystream; Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{li2018entropy} recovered an equivalent key for an entropy-based
cipher; and the survey of Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{li2019attacker} catalogues a year of such breaks,
nearly all exploiting plaintext-independent diffusion. For the confusion stage alone, Li and
Lo~\cite{li2011optimal} established the optimal number of known/chosen plaintexts needed to invert a
permutation-only multimedia cipher, of order $\log_{L} (MN)$ for $L$ intensity levels---the same
logarithmic regime our attack achieves; the general quantitative theory of permutation-only
cryptanalysis~\cite{Li2008general} bounds this effort, and recent chosen-plaintext breaks continue to
recover equivalent keys from a handful of images~\cite{Ma2020cryptanalysis,Zhao2025cryptanalysis},
confirming the vulnerability remains a live concern. Alvarez and Li~\cite{alvarez2006} set out basic requirements
for chaos-based cryptosystems, including plaintext-dependent keystreams and sensitivity to the
plaintext; LLEO violates these. The differential metrics we scrutinise were standardised by Wu
\emph{et al.}~\cite{wu2011npcr}, who also note that NPCR/UACI quantify sensitivity to a one-pixel
plaintext change---precisely the quantity LLEO fails to deliver despite its reported value.

The pattern is structural, not incidental: when both confusion and diffusion are fixed by the key the
cipher is---as a function of the plaintext---an affine or monomial map, and composing more such stages
changes only its coefficients~\cite{li2019attacker,li2011optimal}. The decisive question is binary: does
any key material depend on the image? Schemes that answer ``no'' (LLEO included) fall to an equivalent
key, while those that bind the keystream to a per-image value resist---both sides of which our positive
control demonstrates on the same construction (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\emph{What is new here.} Prior equivalent-key cryptanalyses---Arroyo \emph{et al.}~\cite{arroyo2009}, Li
\emph{et al.}~\cite{li2018entropy,li2019attacker}---break one named scheme at a time by hand, each analysis
bespoke to its target. Our contribution is not another single break but its \emph{automation}: a black-box
decision procedure that, knowing nothing of a cipher's internals, identifies the position-wise algebra
(affine, XOR, or GF(2)-affine), recovers an equivalent key, and issues a held-out certificate we prove
sound (Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}), then runs unattended across a corpus and four real published
schemes. The few-query bound itself matches the Li--Lo permutation-only optimum~\cite{li2011optimal}; what
is new is the reusable, self-certifying audit and its reach to the bit-level (cellular-automata) designs
that defeat the classical affine/XOR recovery.

\section{The target cipher}\label{sec:target}
LLEO encrypts an $M\times N$ grayscale image $X$ in three key-driven steps.

\emph{(1) Substitution.} $X$ is split into 16 horizontal strips. The eight odd strips are combined
with a keystream $k_{\mathrm{log}}$ produced by the logistic map $x_{n+1}=r\,x_n(1-x_n)$, and the
eight even strips with a keystream $k_{\mathrm{lor}}$ produced by the Lorenz system
$\dot x=\sigma(y-x),\ \dot y=x(\rho-z)-y,\ \dot z=xy-\beta z$, through an invertible per-position
operation; decryption uses the modular ``conjugate'' of the keystream, i.e.\ a multiplicative inverse.

\emph{(2) Permutation 1.} The 16 horizontal strips are reordered by a Fisher--Yates shuffle (a
standard method for randomly reordering a list).

\emph{(3) Permutation 2.} The result is divided into 16 vertical strips and shuffled again, giving the
ciphertext.

The four ``decryption keys'' are the two keystreams ($d_1,d_2$) and the two shuffle index lists
($d_3,d_4$). The chaotic initial conditions are derived from the secret key and the shuffles are
seeded from it; \emph{no quantity depends on the image $X$}. Although the scheme is repeatedly called
``asymmetric,'' the same four keys both encrypt and decrypt: it is a symmetric cipher.

\section{Cryptanalysis}\label{sec:crypt}
\subsection{Threat model}
We work in the standard chosen-plaintext setting under Kerckhoffs's principle (the attacker knows the
algorithm; only the key is secret): the attacker can obtain the ciphertext of any image it chooses under
a fixed unknown key, and seeks an \emph{equivalent key}---a procedure that decrypts any ciphertext of
the probed size---not the chaotic secret seed itself.
Because the cipher is plaintext-independent, the same equivalent key also follows from enough known
plaintext--ciphertext pairs~\cite{zhang2018improved}; we present the chosen-plaintext version as it is
minimal.

\subsection{Structural observation}
Index the image as a length-$n$ vector, $n=MN$. Each LLEO step is linear over $\mathbb{Z}_{256}$ and
fixed by the key: substitution multiplies coordinate $p$ by a fixed unit $K[p]$ (odd, hence invertible
mod 256), and the two block shuffles compose into one position permutation $P$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial}
C[j] \;=\; K[s(j)]\cdot X[s(j)] \pmod{256},\qquad s=P^{-1},
\end{equation}
a fixed \emph{monomial} map: every ciphertext pixel is a single plaintext pixel scaled by a fixed unit
and relocated. Two consequences are immediate. There is no additive constant, since
$E(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}$. And there is no diffusion: $C[j]$ depends on exactly one input coordinate
$s(j)$---so a one-pixel plaintext change alters exactly one ciphertext pixel, which we exploit in
Section~\ref{sec:results} and visualize in Fig.~\ref{fig:diffusion}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:monomial}
If a permutation--diffusion cipher's keystream and permutations depend only on the key, it realises a
fixed \emph{affine} map of the plaintext: the monomial \eqref{eq:monomial} with each $K[p]$ a unit in
$\mathbb{Z}_{256}$ when the substitution is a modular multiply, or an affine map $C=Mx\oplus b$ over
$\mathrm{GF}(2)$ (with $b=E(\mathbf{0})$ possibly nonzero) when the substitution is XOR-based; composing
any number of such rounds yields a map of the same form. In either case it is recovered from
$1+\lceil\log_{256}n\rceil$ chosen plaintexts: one all-ones (resp.\ all-zeros) query fixes the
substitution at every output position, and $\lceil\log_{256}n\rceil$ index-digit queries fix the
permutation $s$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each stage---multiplication by the key-derived $K$ and the two block shuffles---is a fixed function of
the key applied to pixel values or positions, so any composition is a fixed key-only map; over
$\mathbb{Z}_{256}$ this is the monomial \eqref{eq:monomial}. The keystream is forced odd, so $K[p]$ is a
unit and $x\mapsto K[p]\,x$ is a bijection with inverse $K[p]^{-1}$. Querying $X=\mathbf{1}$ gives
$C[j]=K[s(j)]$; querying the $t$-th base-256 digit of the index gives
$C[j]=K[s(j)]\,\mathrm{digit}_t(s(j))$, and dividing by the recovered $K[s(j)]$ yields
$\mathrm{digit}_t(s(j))$; collecting all digits reconstructs $s$. If instead the substitution is XOR
with a key-only keystream, the same argument over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ gives an affine map $C=Mx\oplus b$
with $b=E(\mathbf{0})$: the all-zeros query returns $b$ and the index-digit queries return $s$, exactly
as in the CBC-like XOR cipher we break in Section~\ref{sec:oracle}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Equivalent-key recovery}
Because $K$ and $s$ are fixed, two kinds of chosen plaintext suffice.

\emph{(a) Multiplier, one query.} Encrypting the all-ones image $\mathbf{1}$ gives, by
\eqref{eq:monomial}, $C[j]=K[s(j)]\cdot 1=K[s(j)]$: the exact multiplier acting at every output
position.

\emph{(b) Permutation, $\lceil\log_{256} n\rceil$ queries.} Let $P_t$ be the plaintext whose pixel $i$
holds the $t$-th base-256 digit of its index $i$. Then $C[j]=K[s(j)]\cdot \mathrm{digit}_t(s(j))$;
multiplying by the already-known $K[s(j)]^{-1}$ yields $\mathrm{digit}_t(s(j))$. Collecting all
$\lceil\log_{256} n\rceil$ digits reconstructs $s(j)$ for every $j$, hence the full permutation.

The recovered pair $(s,K)$ is an equivalent key; any ciphertext is inverted by
$X[s(j)] = K[s(j)]^{-1}\,C[j]$. The total cost is
\begin{equation}
1+\big\lceil\log_{256} n\big\rceil \text{ chosen plaintexts},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ \PH{cam_cpt} for a $512\times512$ image, independent of the nominal key length.

Concretely, a $512\times512$ image needs four queries---one all-ones image and three index-digit
images---after which Algorithm~\ref{alg:attack} returns the full map in a single pass.
Table~\ref{tab:complexity} states the complexity and compares it to the optimal permutation-only bound
of Li and Lo~\cite{li2011optimal}: our query count is order-optimal and the cost is independent of the
cipher's nominal key length, so the advertised keyspace is irrelevant---the equivalent key bypasses key
recovery entirely~\cite{Jolfaei2016permutation}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Equivalent-key recovery for LLEO}
\label{alg:attack}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $\hat{K} \gets \mathrm{Enc}(\mathbf{1})$ \Comment{$\hat{K}[j]=K[s(j)]$: multiplier at output $j$}
\For{$t \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $\lceil\log_{256} n\rceil-1$}
  \State $C \gets \mathrm{Enc}(P_t)$ \Comment{$P_t[i]={}$ the $t$-th base-256 digit of index $i$}
  \State $d_t[j] \gets C[j]\cdot \hat{K}[j]^{-1} \bmod 256$ \textbf{for all} $j$
\EndFor
\State $s[j] \gets \sum_t d_t[j]\,256^{t}$ \Comment{source index for each output $j$}
\State \Return $\mathrm{Dec}(C):\ X[s(j)] \gets \hat{K}[j]^{-1} C[j] \bmod 256$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Why extra structure does not help}
A composition of fixed key-only maps is again one, so extra rounds, chaotic maps, or block shuffles leave
\eqref{eq:monomial} intact (only $K,s$ change) and the attack applies unchanged; it is moreover a
\emph{known}-plaintext attack whenever the available plaintexts span the index digits. The single
property that would defeat it is a keystream that depends on $X$.

\subsection{The break}
The attack of Section~\ref{sec:crypt} recovers the equivalent key in \PH{cam_cpt} chosen plaintexts and
decrypts a fresh ciphertext pixel-exactly in \PH{cam_attack_s}\,s (Cameraman, single core; the random

\subsection{The differential metric is illusory}
LLEO reports NPCR $\approx$99.6\,\% as evidence of differential-attack resistance. But by
\eqref{eq:monomial} flipping one plaintext pixel changes exactly one ciphertext pixel
(Fig.~\ref{fig:diffusion}, left), so the true
one-pixel NPCR is $100/MN=\PH{cam_npcr1px}\,\%$ (UACI \PH{cam_uaci1px}\,\%)---five orders of magnitude
below the reported value. The 99.6\,\% figure instead measures the difference between \emph{unrelated}
images, which for LLEO is \PH{npcr_unrelated}\,\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:diffusion}, centre); this is achieved by
any cipher with a near-uniform
output and says nothing about chosen-plaintext security. Table~\ref{tab:claims} contrasts each claimed
property with what the cipher actually provides. Finally, the chaotic keystreams are themselves
non-uniform (Fig.~\ref{fig:keystream}): a chi-square test (a standard check for whether values are
evenly spread) rejects uniformity for the logistic keystream
($\chi^2=\PH{chi2_logistic}$, $p<10^{-300}$, numerically zero).

\section{A structural oracle: from one break to a reusable audit}\label{sec:oracle}
A single break is an exercise; a reusable test for the structural flaw is the contribution. We package the attack
as a black-box \emph{structural oracle}. Given only encryption access to an unknown cipher on $M\times N$
images---no key, no source---it fits three position-wise models---affine and XOR
(Proposition~\ref{prop:monomial}) and a GF(2)-affine \emph{bitlinear} model (below)---from a handful of
chosen plaintexts and \emph{certifies} the result by predicting the ciphertext of fresh random plaintexts
(Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}). Two cheap signals drive the verdict:
the \emph{avalanche} (fraction of ciphertext bytes a one-pixel plaintext flip changes; ${\approx}1/n$
means no diffusion) and the \emph{recoverability} $R$ (exact held-out ciphertext-prediction rate;
$R{\approx}1$ means broken). We run it blind on both structural archetypes and four real published schemes.

\begin{proposition}[Held-out certificate]\label{prop:certificate}
If $\mathrm{Enc}$ is position-wise (affine, XOR, or GF(2)-affine) and the recovered model $\hat M$ predicts
$t$ independent uniform-random plaintexts exactly, then $\hat M=\mathrm{Enc}$ everywhere: the probes pin
each per-position map and the digit images pin $s$, so $R{=}1$ certifies a true equivalent key. Conversely,
if any per-position map depends on the plaintext (there exist $P,P'$ whose maps differ), no fixed
position-wise model predicts all plaintexts, and $R{\to}1/256$ per byte once the dependence is through a
cryptographic hash or a fresh nonce. The \textup{\textsc{broken}} verdict therefore carries a falsifiable
certificate; \textup{\textsc{resists}} is correct for plaintext- and nonce-bound constructions.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{The bitlinear model.} A key-only cipher can append a per-pixel \emph{bit-level} stage---a cellular
automaton or a bit-sliced S-box---on an XOR/permutation core. Such a stage is a fixed GF(2)-affine byte map
$g(y){=}My\oplus d$ with $M{\neq}I$ (it mixes bits \emph{within} a byte), so the composite is
$C[j]{=}M\,P[s_j]\oplus B_j$. The plain-XOR model then reads $\mathrm{out}\oplus c_0{=}M\,D[s_j]$ instead of
$D[s_j]$, the source index comes back bit-scrambled, and a key-only cipher is mislabelled \textsc{resists}.
The remedy is a third model: eight bit-basis probes (every pixel ${=}\,1{\ll}t$) read $M$ one column at a
time ($\mathrm{out}_t\oplus c_0{=}M e_t$, constant across positions), and $M^{-1}$ over GF(2) unscrambles
the digit images to recover $s$ exactly. It is a strict generalization ($M{=}I$ recovers XOR), so it never
overturns a prior verdict; it widens the cheap-query regime to the bit-level substitution layers common in
this literature.

\textbf{Archetype corpus.} Run blind over \PH{oracle_n_schemes} reconstructed archetypes
(Table~\ref{tab:oracle}, Fig.~\ref{fig:oracle}), the oracle breaks all \PH{oracle_n_keyonly_broken}
\emph{fully} key-only constructions at $R{=}1$ in at most \PH{oracle_max_queries} chosen plaintexts: LLEO's chaos
monomial, a pure permutation, an additive stream, a general affine map, and a modern AES-CTR-style key
stream. The chaos is incidental: an AES-CTR keystream \emph{reused} across images---a fixed key and
counter for every image---is exactly as broken as LLEO. This is a keystream/counter-reuse failure, not
any weakness of AES-CTR used correctly with a fresh per-image nonce (which our nonce-bound entry shows
resists); the flaw is a reused plaintext-independent keystream, not the choice of map. The
\PH{oracle_n_bound_resist} defended schemes---reseeding the keystream from SHA-256 of the plaintext, or
from a per-image nonce---are correctly declined ($R{\approx}$ chance), and there a one-pixel change
avalanches across the whole image. Beyond the corpus, the same equivalent-key CPA also breaks a chained
CBC-like XOR cipher (a structurally different, non-position-wise construction not counted among the
\PH{oracle_n_schemes}) in \PH{gen_cpt} chosen plaintexts, per Proposition~\ref{prop:monomial}. These are reconstructions of structural archetypes; we turn next to real
published schemes.

\textbf{Scope.} The cheap-query regime spans position-wise affine, monomial, XOR, \emph{and} GF(2)-affine
(bitlinear) maps---the last costs only eight extra bit-basis probes (12 chosen plaintexts at $512^2$, as
for LSCM-CA below). The boundary it does not cross is a key-only but \emph{genuinely nonlinear} byte S-box
(not GF(2)-affine): recovering it needs up to $L{=}256$ queries per position to tabulate, so such ciphers
stay structurally broken but fall outside this logarithmic-query regime.

\textbf{Real-literature audit.} We then ran the audit on \PH{pub_n_real} real, open-access 2023--2025
chaos image ciphers, reconstructed faithfully from their published equations/pseudocode
(Table~\ref{tab:published}, Fig.~\ref{fig:published}). The decisive question---does any chaotic seed,
S-box, or permutation index depend on the plaintext?---separates them cleanly. \emph{LSCM-CA}%
~\cite{sun2025lscm}, whose keystream and permutation are key-only and which adds a per-pixel
cellular-automata bit stage, is \textsc{broken} at $R{=}\PH{pub_lscm_R}$ in \PH{pub_lscm_q} chosen
plaintexts---but \emph{only} via the bitlinear model: the affine/XOR model alone returns
$R{=}\PH{pub_lscm_R_naive}$, a false negative its CA layer induces (the real scheme that motivated the
model). The other three bind the keystream to the plaintext and correctly \textsc{resist}
($R{\approx}\PH{pub_li_R}$): \emph{Li--DNA}~\cite{li2024dnadual} seeds its logistic and Chen systems from
SHA-256 of the image, \emph{MILE}~\cite{mansour2025mile} from the sum of the first $8{\times}8$ block, and
\emph{MIEA-PRHM}~\cite{feng2024miea} keeps a key-only keystream but adds a single plaintext-dependent
scalar.

Two of these resistances are \emph{thin}, and the oracle's two signals expose it. \emph{MILE} shows no
diffusion---a one-pixel change outside its seed block moves one byte ($A{\cdot}n{\approx}1$)---yet
$R{\approx}$ chance, because its plaintext-dependence flows through only 64 pixels: hold that seed block
constant (a legitimate chosen-plaintext sub-class) and $R$ rises to \PH{pub_mile_fix_off} off the block,
collapsing it to a recovered fixed map. \emph{MIEA-PRHM} avalanches fully yet injects just 8 bits of
plaintext entropy (a 256-way scalar). The oracle certifies ``\emph{not} a fixed plaintext-independent
map,'' which is true; it does \emph{not} certify strength, and these two signals cannot separate
MIEA-PRHM's near-miss from Li--DNA's genuine SHA-256 binding (Fig.~\ref{fig:published})---a boundary we
mark rather than hide. Reconstructions are paper-only (no scheme released code); each verdict is
nonetheless a falsifiable certificate of the reconstructed cipher. (Two further
candidates~\cite{lone2023ahc,guvenoglu2024mlc} are deferred pending full text.)

\textbf{Invertibility (the odd-multiplier question).} The recovery needs each multiplier $K[p]$ to be a
unit mod 256---and this is \emph{guaranteed by LLEO}, not assumed by us: the scheme decrypts via a
modular conjugate, which forces the keystream odd. On the reconstruction every recovered multiplier is
odd and $E(\mathbf 0){=}\mathbf 0$ holds exactly. Were even multipliers allowed, $x\mapsto K[p]\,x$ would
be $2$-to-$1$ there; the oracle does not return a wrong inverse but degrades gracefully, reporting the
invertible fraction and a partial recoverability (on a deliberately even-tolerant variant, about half the
positions are invertible and $R{=}\PH{even_recoverability}$).

\textbf{Falsifiability of the LLEO claim.} Our attack rests on the load-bearing claim that no LLEO quantity
depends on the image; the oracle makes that claim \emph{falsifiable}, not merely asserted
(Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}). Matching the
paper's entropy or correlation cannot establish it---countless ciphers hit those values---but the
held-out test can: an equivalent key recovered from \PH{cam_cpt} chosen plaintexts predicts the
ciphertext of fresh random images \emph{exactly}, which is possible only under plaintext-independence. If
a reader's reading of the original differs (say, some keystream were image-seeded), the same oracle
returns RESISTS on that variant, as it does for our SHA-256--seeded control---so the verdict is checkable
against any reimplementation, including the authors' own.

\section{Discussion}
LLEO illustrates a persistent failure mode: \emph{good statistics are not security}. A flat histogram,
near-ideal entropy, near-zero correlation, and a large inter-image NPCR are all satisfied by our fixed,
key-only map, which we nonetheless break in \PH{cam_cpt} queries; testing plaintext sensitivity
directly~\cite{alvarez2006,wu2011npcr} is what catches this. (The scheme's ``asymmetric'' label is also a
misnomer---the same keys encrypt and decrypt---but that is cosmetic.)

The remedies are standard. (i) Make the keystream depend on the plaintext---e.g.\ derive the chaotic
initial conditions from a SHA-256 hash of the image, so a different plaintext yields an unrelated
keystream. (ii) Add genuine diffusion, e.g.\ a chaining rule $C[i]=f(X[i],k[i],C[i-1])$, so one
plaintext change propagates. With either, \eqref{eq:monomial} is no longer fixed across images and the
attack fails: it recovers \PH{keyonly_match_pct}\,\% of pixels against the key-only cipher but only
\PH{defended_match_pct}\,\% (chance) once the keystream is SHA-256--seeded from the image.

\subsection{Limitations}
Three bounds on our claims. \emph{(i) Equivalent key, not the seed.} We recover an equivalent key that
decrypts any ciphertext of the probed size, not the underlying chaotic secret seed---the standard,
defensible claim in this literature~\cite{li2019attacker}. \emph{(ii) The real-corpus verdicts are
reconstruction-dependent.} The LLEO break uses the real construction and is validated against the paper's
reported statistics (Section~\ref{sec:results}); the four real-corpus verdicts (Table~\ref{tab:published})
rest on paper-only reconstructions---no scheme released code---at the confidence marked per row
(Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}), and two further candidates are deferred pending full text. Each is a
falsifiable certificate of the \emph{reconstructed} cipher, checkable against any reimplementation
including the authors' own (Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}). \emph{(iii) The cheap-query regime has a
boundary.} It covers affine, XOR, and GF(2)-affine maps; a key-only but genuinely nonlinear byte S-box
stays structurally broken but needs up to $256$ queries per position, outside the logarithmic regime.

\emph{Disclosure.} Consistent with responsible-disclosure practice, the authors of every scheme we
break---LLEO~\cite{jain2025lleo} and the reconstructed LSCM-CA~\cite{sun2025lscm}---will be notified
before camera-ready, and our reproduction and attack code is available at \coderepo.

\section{Conclusion}
A 2025 chaos-based image cipher is completely broken by an equivalent-key chosen-plaintext attack in
\PH{cam_cpt} chosen plaintexts and a fraction of a second, despite near-ideal entropy and a reported
NPCR of $\approx$99.6\,\%. The break follows directly from the cipher being key-only; its differential
metric, measured correctly, is \PH{cam_npcr1px}\,\%. We recommend plaintext-dependent keystreams and
real diffusion, and that statistical scores accompany---rather than replace---an explicit
chosen-plaintext security argument. More broadly, the attack generalizes into a reusable structural audit
that decides plaintext-independence, with a held-out certificate we prove sound: it recovers an
equivalent key from every key-only archetype, and on four real published 2023--2025 schemes it breaks the
one key-only design and clears three that bind the keystream to the plaintext---one break needing a
GF(2)-affine extension that widens the audit to bit-level diffusion.
